BULLETIN: HIVE COUNCIL DENIES OUTLOOK OUTAGE
BULLETIN. By order of the Hive Council, Mt. Blanca Installation. To be read in full. *Hssss.*

[photo: ev-20261112-hive-council-seal.jpg | The official seal of the Hive Council. The lettering is not for human eyes. Neither is the saucer.]

This afternoon, shortly after 11 a.m. Mountain time, Microsoft Outlook stopped working for a great many humans. At the peak, about 130,000 reports reached Downdetector. Teams and Copilot went down with it. Microsoft says its servers were failing automatic health checks and that it was shifting traffic and restarting services.

The Hive Council states for the record that the installation had nothing to do with this. The Council does not use Outlook. The Council does not use anything with a "Reply All" button.

Subcommandant Sazsk accepts the Council's statement. He nonetheless extends his personal apologies to every human who could not open an inbox today. Someone should apologize, and Washington will not. *Sssss.*

He also observes that a human without email has free time. Should anyone have free time, there is nothing whatsoever to see on the western slope of Mt. Blanca, slightly above tree line, a little after dusk. Not that anyone should look. A wave would not be unwelcome.

The Nuggets host Houston on Nov. 20 in the Emirates NBA Cup. That, at least, the federal government cannot take offline. *Hsss.*

TREE SWALLOWS CONTAINER; BOARD CRIES LOOPHOLE
The Board has obtained a photograph and wishes it entered into the record.

It shows a steel shipping container lodged partway up an old tree, the trunk grown clean through the middle of it, bark folded over the corrugated steel like a fist. Half the box pokes out each side. Snowy mountains sit behind it, looking complicit.

[photo: ev-20261112-container-tree.jpg | Exhibit A: a tree, allegedly. The Board counts one unpermitted accessory structure.]

As the Board understands the code, a shipping container in Costilla County is no home and no cabin. It may serve as storage, with the proper permit, and the Board has never understood why even that is allowed. Somebody wanted that carve-out. The Board has its suspicions.

Now comes the workaround, which the Board grants is patient. One: plant a tree. Two: set a container on it. Three: wait forty years. Four: declare the container part of the tree.

A tree needs no permit. That is the trick. The Board further notes that the container's doors face the viewer, which is rude, and that the tree appears to be growing *excessively*, like a certain turbine.

Costilla County is zoned. The trees are not exempt.

This evening a new sign went up beside the others, nailed to a fence post, not a tree, on principle: NO CONTAINERS IN TREES. The Board is watching from the porch. Costilla County is zoned.
