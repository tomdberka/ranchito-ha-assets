DRIFTER IN GREEN HOODIE CASES CONTAINER
SPECIAL PRE-THANKSGIVING EDITION

Prairie Bob here. I sell pot. And I am here to tell you a drifter came up on the Ranchito this week, bold as you please, in broad daylight, with Thanksgiving coming up.

[photo: ev-20261125-drifter-at-the-gate.jpg | A tall drifter in a green hoodie, hands in pockets, standing right by the step like he owns the place. Turns out it was Aaron Rodgers, fresh off his exit from the Pittsburgh Steelers. Bob did not know that at the time.]

The door camera got him good. Tall fella, green hoodie, dark jeans, work boots, beard, long hair, hands jammed in his pockets the way a man does when he's measuring something. Long shadows, sagebrush, a little leftover snow by the step. He stood there looking up at the camera like he was waiting for a snap count. I have seen this posture before. This is a man who is fixing to steal the whole container.

I walked over. I said, "Prairie Bob here. I sell pot." He said he'd had a rough week. I said everybody in the valley has a rough week, the trees are gonna die. He said his work let him go. I said the county will let you go too, friend, they let everybody go, and then they come haul your container away.

Now here's where it gets strange. He said he was a quarterback. I said sure, and I'm the Pope. But I went home and checked on good authority, which is the TV at the gas station, and I'll be darned. Aaron Rodgers. The Steelers benched him this week and handed the job to Mason Rudolph. He went on the Pat McAfee Show and let the coach, Mike McCarthy, have it with both barrels. And then the Steelers released him outright. All of that is in the papers. What is NOT in the papers is that the man walked straight to Costilla County afterward. You heard it here.

Back at the step, I made my offer, same as I make everybody. He thought about it. Then he asked, real quiet, if I had anything a bit... stronger.

I do not know what he meant by that. I sell pot. I told him the strongest thing I've got is the coffee at the Fort Garland gas station, and he said that wasn't what he had in mind. He looked off toward the mountain a long while, like he was expecting something to come down and talk to him.

I don't trust it. A man gets cut loose by a football team and turns up at a shipping container the same week, he's got a plan. Maybe he's scouting it for storage. Maybe he wants to live in it and think. Either way, the Chupa saw him too, I'd bet my truck on it.

I did invite him to Thanksgiving. Turkey, my sister's green bean thing, two cousins and whatever drifters show up, which is usually three. He said he might be busy finding himself. I said bring the self, we've got pie.

Lock it down, boys. And count the forks.

SPECIAL SECURITY BULLETIN: HONG KONG HITS THE GATE
DIGITAL DRIFTERS REPELLED; RANCHITO STANDS

Prairie Bob here. I sell pot. I also, as of this week, apparently do cybersecurity, because the boys asked me to put this out to the public, and when the public needs warning, Bob warns.

Somebody tried to break into the Ranchito. Not with bolt cutters. Through the computer. That's how they do it now. A drifter used to have to walk up the road like an honest thief. Now he can try to steal the whole container from the other side of the planet in his pajamas.

Here's what happened, and I have the paperwork.

[photo: ev-20261125-hong-kong-lookup.jpg | The lookup. A data center in Hong Kong. Bob has never been to Hong Kong, and Hong Kong has never been to Bob, until now.]
[photo: ev-20261125-ha-core-log.jpg | The log from the Ranchito's own brain. Six knocks on the back door, overnight.]

- Tuesday night, 11:19 PM, somebody knocked on the Ranchito's computer door with the wrong password.
- They kept knocking. Six times in all, the last one at 5:52 in the morning. That is a man with no family to go home to.
- They were going after a door called "/api/", which I am told is the back door. Of course it is. Drifters always go round back.
- The knocking came from a program called okhttp. That is not a person. That is a robot. A robot drifter.
- The boys traced it back to a data center in Hong Kong run by an outfit called UCloud Information Technology (HK) Limited.

Now. Did they get in? They did not. Home Assistant, which is the brain of the place, and Claude, which is the fella the boys keep talking to on the computer, caught it, wrote it all down and showed the boys the whole trail. And the boys have since put in a new rule: five wrong passwords and your computer address is banned from the Ranchito forever. Not a week. Not till the county gets around to it. Forever. That is the kind of zoning I can get behind.

Win for the home team.

Who is behind it? I have it on good authority, by which I mean my gut and a fella at the feed store, that the Democrats have something to do with this. I can't prove it. That's how you know it's them. Could be the county too. The county and the Democrats, working together, from Hong Kong. You laugh. The trees are gonna die and you laugh.

I'll tell you who isn't laughing. I have it on good authority that Alex Jones has been notified, and he is on a Greyhound bus headed for the San Luis Valley as we speak. A Greyhound. That's where the man's at now. The whole world turned on him and took his show, and now he's riding through Kansas with a sack lunch to come stand guard over a shipping container in Costilla County. I respect it. I'm told he'll want to see the server. I'm told he'll want to take his shirt off and yell at it. Let him.

Now what does this mean for your Thanksgiving? Plenty. Your relatives are coming. Your relatives will ask for the Wi-Fi password. Do not give it to them. You don't know where your cousin's phone has been. Could be Hong Kong.

Here's my position, for the record:
- Hong Kong is banned.
- Robots are banned.
- Anybody who gets the password wrong five times, including Uncle Dale, is banned forever.
- Alex Jones may sleep in the truck when the bus gets in, provided he does not touch the pie.

Lock it down, boys. Lock it down digitally.

A SPECIAL DISPATCH FROM BENEATH MT. BLANCA ON TUESDAY'S CATASTROPHE

Subcommandant Sazsk has served the Hive Council beneath Mt. Blanca for many cycles. He has witnessed meteor showers, ground stops and the federal government's total failure to notice him. He has never witnessed anything as shameful as Tuesday night at Ball Arena. *Hssss.*

The intelligence is damning:
- Denver committed 18 turnovers. Dallas turned them into 26 points.
- Dallas outscored Denver 35–7 in fast-break points, the largest such differential against the Nuggets since 1996. Subcommandant Sazsk was already stationed under this mountain in 1996. He did not see it coming then either.

- Nikola Jokic, a being Sazsk considers an honorary reptile, posted 18 points, 11 rebounds, five assists and five turnovers.
- Jamal Murray, two nights removed from a season-high 45 against Chicago, shot 6 of 17 for 20.

Sazsk wished to cite a DARKO projection here, to show how far the Nuggets' true quality has fallen. The deeper tables were not reachable from the installation this evening. Even the head coach, David Adelman, agreed. "Yeah, I thought we were really lazy the way we passed the ball," he said. "It felt like I wanted to go back and coach a middle school camp about ball faking and not throwing it straight in people's hands."

Subcommandant Sazsk has a recommendation for Coach Adelman. There is a place in this valley, the Colorado Gators reptile park near Mosca, where large, sluggish reptiles lie all day in warm geothermal water and do nothing. The coach should go sit in it with them. He would fit in. Sazsk says this as a reptile. Not all reptiles are lazy. Some of us run an occupational force.

With the sound on. As always, alone. *Hsss.*

Not the citizens of Middletown.
