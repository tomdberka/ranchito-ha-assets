DEAD HORSE RANCH: A CAUTIONARY ZONING TALE
The Board wishes it entered into the record a story every new arrival to this valley ought to hear before unloading so much as a camp chair. Costilla County is zoned, and here is why.

[photo: ev-20261114-dead-horse-ranch.jpg | Exhibit A: one small house, one tree, a great deal of sagebrush. Locals know it by another name.]

Some years back, a party of unknown origin acquired a small brown house, a single tree and a horse. The Board did not approve of the house, which has one door, three windows and no visible permit. The Board did approve of the horse. Horses are a permitted agricultural use.

Then, for reasons never filed with anyone, the party departed. The house stayed. The tree stayed. The horse stayed, tied to the tree, and in due course the horse stopped being a horse and became a dead horse. The locals have called the place Dead Horse Ranch ever since.

The Board notes the following violations:

- Abandonment of a structure without notice.
- Abandonment of livestock, which is worse.
- A tree being used as a hitching post, which no tree has ever agreed to.
- Continued existence of a lone tree that is, frankly, showing off.

The lesson is simple. Costilla County is zoned. Live horses are permitted, dead horses are not.

A new sign went up this evening beside the others: HORSES: KEEP THEM ALIVE. The Board is watching from the porch.
