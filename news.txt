TREE SWALLOWS CONTAINER; BOARD CRIES LOOPHOLE
The Board has obtained a photograph and wishes it entered into the record.

It shows a steel shipping container lodged partway up an old tree, the trunk grown clean through the middle of it, bark folded over the corrugated steel like a fist. Half the box pokes out each side. Snowy mountains sit behind it, looking complicit.

[photo: ev-20261112-container-tree.jpg | Exhibit A: a tree, allegedly. The Board counts one unpermitted accessory structure.]

As the Board understands the code, a shipping container in Costilla County is no home and no cabin. It may serve as storage, with the proper permit, and the Board has never understood why even that is allowed. Somebody wanted that carve-out. The Board has its suspicions.

Now comes the workaround, which the Board grants is patient. One: plant a tree. Two: set a container on it. Three: wait forty years. Four: declare the container part of the tree.

A tree needs no permit. That is the trick. The Board further notes that the container's doors face the viewer, which is rude, and that the tree appears to be growing *excessively*, like a certain turbine.

Costilla County is zoned. The trees are not exempt.

This evening a new sign went up beside the others, nailed to a fence post, not a tree, on principle: NO CONTAINERS IN TREES. The Board is watching from the porch. Costilla County is zoned.
